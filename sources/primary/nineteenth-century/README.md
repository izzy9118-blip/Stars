# Nineteenth-Century Primary Sources

This directory continues the chronological History of Power repair after the European early-modern corpus.

The first production unit is the Napoleonic administrative state: the conversion of revolutionary sovereignty and mass mobilization into durable executive, territorial, legal, educational, military, informational, and police capacities. The corpus then follows the survival of that machinery through regime change, the rival constitutional and class claims of 1848, national unification, and the Paris Commune's alternative reconstruction of republican power in 1871.

Rules:

- Primary texts govern over later summaries.
- Original-language and official documentary witnesses govern where available.
- Later transcriptions, editorial collections, and translations remain distinct witness classes.
- No claim about the administrative state is admitted merely because a later historian calls an institution centralized, bureaucratic, national, or modern.
- Constitutional power, administrative practice, military mobilization, legal codification, schooling, policing, statistics, and correspondence are mapped separately before synthesis.
- The Napoleonic layer does not begin ex nihilo: Revolutionary institutions such as conscription are preserved where Napoleon inherited and regularized them.
- The existing History of Power narrative remains untouched during acquisition and passage mapping.
- Rousseau, Kant, Hegel, Marx, and Tocqueville remain distinct teachings; their disagreements are not converted into one doctrine of modernization.
- Constitutional nation, centralized administration, class revolution, and federated commune remain rival political forms even when one is defeated.
